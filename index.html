Judging criteria

These are the eight areas the arrangement should deliberately demonstrate.

Strategy: make each criterion audible or visible. Technical ability should serve the song; originality should feel intentional, and coordination should be obvious from clean starts, endings and transitions.

Arrangement principles

1 · Don't overfill the mix

At any moment, choose a lead vocal, a rhythmic foundation and one or two supporting textures. Give instruments a featured moment instead of letting every player play continuously.

2 · Choose by the actual singer

A song that suits someone's tone may not suit their range. Assign sections by comfort, pitch accuracy, diction, stamina and style—not just gender or who sings the original.

3 · Verify before locking a mashup

A matching mood or BPM does not guarantee compatible notes. Check tonic, melody, phrase endings, rhythmic cycle and vocal range on your actual instruments.

Final rehearsal check

Musical compatibility check

This tool stores your notes; it does not falsely label songs compatible without analysing actual audio or notation.

Before approving a mashup, check: (1) tonic/key and singer range; (2) actual melody notes and phrase endings; (3) raga-defining phrases and gamakam; (4) BPM and beat unit; (5) tala / groove accents; (6) lyric meter and syllable stress; (7) transition rehearsed on keyboard/guitar; (8) final recording sounds clean.
